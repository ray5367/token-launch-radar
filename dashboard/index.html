How the value is figured

One function scores every row. No protocol name is special-cased, no row is hand-picked, and every input is a figure the snapshot itself carries. The three constants that shape the price — the shrinkage prior, the growth weight and the size slope — are fitted by the program on the snapshot's token protocols and tested on a held-out split; the numbers below are the fitted values and the test's results, rendered from the same data file as the board.

  1. Price each category from the market, credibility-weighted. For each category, take every protocol in the snapshot whose token already trades and divide its market cap by its TVL. The category median is the median of those ratios — medians, so one outlier cannot move the price — winsorized to ×0.03…×1.25 before blending. The row's multiple then blends that median with the global median: multiple = w × category median + (1 − w) × global median, where w = n / (n + 21) and n is the category's token-protocol count. The prior 21 is the fitted value: the holdout test below chose it over 1…34. A category with 21 priced protocols earns 50% weight on its own evidence; one with a single protocol earns under 5% and leans on the global median; a category with none prices purely at the global median, and the row says which.
  2. Reward momentum, capped. Seven-day TVL change is clamped to −50%…+100% and weighted: growth factor = 1 + 0.75 × clamped change. The weight 0.75 is fitted, not asserted. A protocol shrinking 50% keeps 0.625×; one doubling gets 1.75×. No report means no change.
  3. Reward a long no-token track record, on a log scale. Age is the days from first listing to the snapshot date, capped at 1,095 days. Age factor = 0.65 + 0.35 × ln(1 + age) / ln(1 + cap): the first year counts more than the fourth. A missing listing date uses the eligible set's median age, figured from the snapshot.
  4. Scale for size: market caps grow slower than TVL. Across the snapshot's token protocols, doubling TVL multiplies the market cap by about 2^0.5, not 2× — big protocols carry lower market-cap/TVL ratios. The size factor is (TVL / median token TVL)^(slope − 1) with the fitted slope 0.5: a protocol ten times the median TVL is priced at about a third of the linear multiple, one a tenth of it at about three times. The board's TVL range sits inside the token protocols' range, so the slope is applied, never extrapolated past the fitted population.
  5. Multiply and rank. implied = TVL × multiple × growth factor × age factor × size factor, rounded to whole dollars. Ties break by slug, so the ranking is stable and reproducible.

The held-out test

The test, in full, is rendered here from the program's own validation record.

Uncertainty on every estimate

Each row's band is the 10th–90th percentile of 200 bootstrap refits: the token protocols' ratios are resampled within their categories with a fixed seed, the medians are recomputed, and the row is repriced with the fitted constants held fixed. The band and the rank range (the row's best and worst rank across the refits) sit beside every value on the board and in the detail view. A row priced from a thin category swings wide; a row priced from a deep one stays put. The seed is fixed, so the bands reproduce exactly.

What the test does not show

The errors are large in absolute terms: the typical held-out prediction lands within about ×2.9 of the true market cap, because market-cap/TVL ratios vary widely even within a category. The bands carry that honestly instead of hiding it. The mean error favours heavier shrinkage than the median criterion chose — the model prices by medians throughout, so the median is the stated selection target, and both errors are reported. Leave-one-out over all 128 token protocols under the chosen constants is reported in the test record too; it is not the deciding test, because the constants were selected on 103 of those 128 — the held-out 25, which touched neither selection nor fitting, are.

What is left out, and why

Excluded from the ranking: any protocol that records a token (a market cap, a CoinGecko id, or a symbol), and any wrapped, staked, bridged or versioned product — Bridge, Canonical Bridge, Liquid Staking, Liquid Restaking, Restaked BTC and CEX categories, names carrying a whole word wrapped, staked or staking, names ending in a version marker such as V2, and names beginning with an exchange name. A wrapped product of a token that already trades is not a launch waiting to happen.

Farm plans, netted

Each row's plan is generated from that protocol's own category, chains, TVL and age: the category picks the venue and the risk, the TVL picks the capital, the first chain picks the gas budget, the age picks the holding period. Steps name amounts, costs and time. They are the standard farming playbook for the category and chain — the snapshot carries no points program, and no plan claims one exists. Each plan is then netted to a verdict: sunk gas (two gas budgets, farming plus exit) plus a stated 5% risk charge on the deployed capital, set against the row's implied value, gives the allocation that breaks even. A single farmer's allocation is plausibly around 0.001% of the implied value — a single-digit percent of supply split among thousands of wallets — so farm needs a break-even share at most 0.001% with the band's low end holding three quarters of the value; thin needs at most 0.01% with the low end holding half; anything else is a skip. The detail view shows the arithmetic, not just the word.
